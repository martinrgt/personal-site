> How would you react to your child's mother ending her own life? Would you make the same choices I made? Would you feel the same regret? How would you support your child through this painful moment of life?

### An expressive game
*Lie in my Heart* is a visual novel that invites the player to live a traumatic episode of the author's life. The game is short, intense and striking.

Sébastien Genvo is a french researcher at the [expressive game lab](https://www.expressivegame.com/gamelab/) in Metz. He focuses on how game mechanics can help game developers and players express themselves and expose their own traumas. *Lie in y Heart* is not only a personal story. It is also a creative research project.

*Lie in my Heart* was nominated for the Ping Awards as best narrative game and was among "Les Inrock"'s top 10 best games of 2019.

[💻 Play on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1116490/Lie_In_My_Heart/)

### Localizing a visual novel
Back in 2018, I was still a PhD candidate, working on my dissertation about Italian literature and video games. As I contacted Sébastien Genvo to get some insight on the important topics in the game studies field, he offered me to curate the italian localization for his upcoming game.

As an Italian Literature PhD student, I felt grateful to be able to work on a video game project. It was the first piece of software I'd ever put my hands on. It made me realize how complex and fascinating making a video game is.

Localizing an expressive visual novel is no easy task. Finding the right tone in Italian to fit the french writing did leave me a few challenges along the way. Translating the cat's name, for example, required some thinking: the French name is "Chaussette" (sock), because the cat has white legs that look like socks. I couldn't resolve in translating it to "Calza" or "Calzino", it just didn't sound as soft and cute. Instead, I went for "Farinella" (from "farina", flour), as if the cat stepped into flour turning its paws white.

### Working with Ren'Py
I localized the game directly with the translation file on Ren'Py. All was on a long text document with translation keys to fill so that they match the French version.

I remember translating the same lines several times. It led me to explore other scripting tools like Twine or Ink, even if I'm sure there are better way to deal with narrative bottlenecks in Ren'Py.
